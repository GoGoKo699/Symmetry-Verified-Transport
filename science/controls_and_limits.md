# Controls, exceptions and resource boundaries

The gain–acceptance law concerns a specific operation: repeat ideal measurements of **total occupation**, keep only half-filled outcomes, and use no correcting feedback. It does not limit every way of protecting or estimating a density signal. Simple changes of control or system size change the conclusion.

## Allowed experiment and charged resources

There are $`L=2N`$ sites. For the density-information experiment, initial states are diagonal in the half-filled sector $`\Omega_N`$; the conditional-channel and attenuation results also allow coherent balanced-sector inputs. Ideal transport is generated by nearest-neighbor random SWAPs with rate $`D`$ per edge; independent symmetric bit flips have rate $`\gamma`$ per site. A check measures total occupation without resolving individual occupations. Every finite predetermined cadence and randomized mixture of cadences is included; the schedule label may be retained.

A failed attempt supplies its charge outcome and failure time, but no microscopic configuration. A retained attempt supplies its entire final bitstring and arbitrary classical processing. This grants more than a single measured density moment.

| Resource or operation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Initialized attempts, including rejected attempts | Every attempt is charged. Acceptance $`P`$ requires an average $`1/P`$ initializations per retained trajectory. |
| Evolution time $`T`$ | Equal observation time for checked and raw experiments. Early rejection may shorten a failed attempt but does not erase its initialization. |
| Global-charge checks | Perfect, instantaneous and nondestructive mathematically; implementation cost is additional. |
| Preparation and final measurement | Required in both experiments; no hardware gate, energy or latency estimate is asserted. |
| Noise calibration | The raw estimator uses supplied $`\gamma`$. An unknown rate changes the statistical experiment. |
| Coherent encoding/recovery, protected memory, corrective feedback and local syndromes | Outside this control class. |

A nondestructive global measurement may require ancillas, routing and coherent storage. The lower bound grants ideal checks. A faulty implementation need not have the same conditional kernel; check faults are not modeled by assuming that they can only hurt.

## A coherent encoding preserves an amplitude at every size

An explicit construction prevents extending the theorem to arbitrary charge-preserving coherent controls. Let $`\Pi_N`$ be the half-filling projector, $`d_N=\binom{2N}{N}`$, $`\mu_N=\Pi_N/d_N`$, and

```math
Z_1=2n_1-I,\qquad X_{\mathrm{all}}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{2N}X_j.
```

Bit complementation preserves half filling, so $`[X_{\mathrm{all}},\Pi_N]=0`$. Every site permutation $`W_\pi`$, including every transport SWAP, satisfies

```math
W_\pi\Pi_NW_\pi^\dagger=\Pi_N,\qquad
W_\pi X_{\mathrm{all}}W_\pi^\dagger=X_{\mathrm{all}}.
```

Thus both $`\mu_N`$ and $`\mu_NX_{\mathrm{all}}`$ are fixed by ideal random-SWAP dynamics. If $`\mathcal E_p`$ denotes independent bit flips of probability $`p`$, commutation with every error operator gives

```math
\Pi_N\mathcal E_p(\mu_N)\Pi_N=P_N(p)\mu_N,
```

```math
\Pi_N\mathcal E_p(\mu_NX_{\mathrm{all}})\Pi_N
=P_N(p)\mu_NX_{\mathrm{all}}.
```

The normalized accepted channel therefore fixes

```math
\rho_u^{\mathrm{enc}}=\mu_N(I+uX_{\mathrm{all}}),
\qquad |u|\le1.
```

The encoding is explicit within the sector. Pair every string $`x`$ with its complement $`\bar x`$, choosing $`x_1=1`$ as representative. On each pair apply

```math
U|x\rangle=\frac{|x\rangle+|\bar x\rangle}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
U|\bar x\rangle=\frac{|x\rangle-|\bar x\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
```

Extend $`U`$ by identity outside the sector. Then

```math
[U,\Pi_N]=0,\qquad U Z_1 U^\dagger=X_{\mathrm{all}}
\quad\text{on }\Omega_N.
```

Encoding $`\mu_N(I+uZ_1)`$, running noisy transport with a final global check, and decoding by $`U^\dagger`$ restores its initial amplitude on the retained branch, for every $`N\ge1`$.

The amplitude has moved into off-diagonal coherence, outside the diagonal harmonic attenuation theorem. This protects an amplitude family; it does **not** implement the original diffusive density evolution. The encoded operator is fixed by ideal SWAP dynamics too, so decoding restores the initial profile rather than the unencoded SSEP-evolved profile. No efficient local synthesis of $`U`$, arbitrary-state quantum memory, or estimation of an unknown transport coefficient follows.

## Two sites are a genuine exception

The local information comparison requires $`N\ge2`$. For $`N=1`$, take $`f=x_1-x_2`$, whose sector variance is one. Let $`h(T)`$ be its ideal transport factor and $`s=e^{-2\gamma T}`$. A single final check has

```math
P_1=\frac{1+s^2}{2},\qquad A_1=\frac{s}{P_1},
\qquad I_{\mathrm{final}}(0)=\frac{2s^2}{1+s^2}\,h(T)^2.
```

This equals the raw density-moment information. The raw full-bitstring information is also equal: outcomes $`00`$ and $`11`$ have no amplitude dependence and $`f=0`$ on them.

Repeated checks can improve it. With $`s_i=e^{-2\gamma\Delta_i}`$,

```math
\frac{I_{\mathrm{check}}(0)}{h(T)^2}
=\prod_i\frac{2s_i^2}{1+s_i^2}
\le\prod_i s_i=s.
```

The inequality is $`2s_i\le1+s_i^2`$. Equally spaced checks approach equality as spacing tends to zero at fixed $`T`$. The frequent-check limit achieves $`s\,h(T)^2`$, strictly larger than the raw value for $`0<s<1`$. A size-independent assertion that verification never improves this information would be false.

## Finer measurements and feedback change the target or resources

Local syndromes can distinguish events that total charge cannot. Correcting detected errors can retain trajectories that postselection discards. Neither operation is included. Coherent channel-verification circuits and feedback are established broader controls; [Tsubouchi et al.](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#channel-verification-and-correction) address them.

Finer occupation measurements need not preserve transport. Resolving every site and rejecting every occupation change rejects the random-SWAP moves themselves. For coherent transport, sufficiently frequent site measurements also induce Zeno suppression. Block-charge checks can alter currents across block boundaries. Such protocols need their own specification of the ideal moves, retained records and physical target.

The factorization also has a boundary: homogeneous independent flips commute with permutations and global charge commutes with SWAPs. An arbitrary interacting number-conserving Hamiltonian need not commute with the flip generator. The result is not an all-Hamiltonian verification theorem.

## Transport inference can use other observables and times

The information theorem estimates a small **initial density-wave amplitude** at fixed observation time, locally at zero amplitude. It does not say that a diffusion coefficient is globally unidentifiable. The [finite-sample result](finite_sample.md) also bounds arbitrary estimators over the explicitly stated weak-amplitude interval, whose width may depend on system size.

For rates of the form

```math
R(k)=R(0)+D_{\mathrm{eff}}k^2,
```

two wavevectors can remove the wavevector-independent term. The two-rate strategy and noise-dependent transport coefficients are already part of [Singh et al.](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#transport-coefficients-and-noise). Shorter windows, another initial ensemble or independently calibrated noise can change the complete inference task.

The restoration cost $`\exp[\Omega(\gamma L^3/D)]`$ applies when restoring an order-one accepted mode at $`T=\Theta(L^2/D)`$ in the stated growing-exponent regime. It does not lower-bound every method of learning $`D`$.

If $`\gamma`$ is unknown, the charge/failure record can contain nuisance-parameter information even though it contains no $`u`$ information at fixed $`\gamma`$. A joint comparison must specify and charge calibration data available to both protocols.
