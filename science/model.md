# Physical model and observation contract

The results concern global charge verification in a specified noisy transport experiment. They compare the density information retained by postselection with the resources spent on all initialized runs. The exact conditional channel is derived in [Conditional dynamics](conditional_dynamics.md); [Cadence and acceptance](cadence_tradeoff.md) proves the tradeoff for every allowed check schedule.

## Spins, charge and transport

Let $`L=2N`$ with $`N\geq1`$. A site has occupation basis $`\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}`$, occupation operator $`n_j=|1\rangle\langle1|_j`$, bit-flip operator $`X_j=|0\rangle\langle1|+|1\rangle\langle0|`$, and density contrast $`Z_j=2n_j-I`$. Define

```math
Q=\sum_{j=1}^{L}n_j,\qquad
\Pi_q=\mathbf 1_{\{Q=q\}},\qquad
\Omega_N=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{2N}:|x|=N\}.
```

The input is an arbitrary density operator supported on the balanced sector:

```math
\rho=\Pi_N\rho\Pi_N.
```

The kernel and attenuation statements apply to every such input, including coherent states. The density-amplitude information experiment uses a particular diagonal family around the uniform balanced-sector state.

On a periodic chain, let $`S_{j,j+1}`$ exchange neighboring sites, with site indices interpreted modulo $`L`$. For $`L\geq4`$, each undirected nearest-neighbor edge is counted once. For the two-site boundary case, use the single edge; the bulk Fourier-rate formula below is stated only for $`L\geq4`$. Ideal transport is the random-unitary Lindblad semigroup with generator

```math
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tr}}(\rho)
 =D\sum_{\{j,j+1\}}
 \bigl(S_{j,j+1}\rho S_{j,j+1}^{\dagger}-\rho\bigr),
 \qquad D>0.
```

The adjoint generator has the same expression on observables. On density observables and diagonal states, this is the symmetric simple exclusion process: neighboring occupations exchange at rate $`D`$. The generator is defined by stochastic SWAP jumps; it is not the Hamiltonian commutator generated by a sum of SWAP operators.

Independent symmetric bit flips occur at rate $`\gamma\geq0`$ on every site:

```math
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{fl}}(\rho)
 =\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{2N}(X_j\rho X_j-\rho).
```

Both rates have units of inverse time. Over an interval $`\Delta\geq0`$, a single spin has net flip probability

```math
s(\Delta)=e^{-2\gamma\Delta},\qquad
p(\Delta)=\frac{1-s(\Delta)}2.
```

The factor two follows from the stated generator: a Poisson number of flips of mean $`\gamma\Delta`$ is odd with probability $`p(\Delta)`$. It is not a separate convention for the flip rate.

The full noisy generator is $`\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tr}}+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{fl}}`$. Its permutation symmetry makes the following factorization exact.

### Why transport and noise commute

For every site permutation $`\sigma`$, its unitary $`U_\sigma`$ satisfies

```math
U_\sigma X_jU_\sigma^\dagger=X_{\sigma(j)}.
```

The sum of all single-site flip generators therefore commutes with conjugation by every site permutation. In particular,

```math
[\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tr}},\mathcal L_{\mathrm{fl}}]=0,\qquad
e^{\Delta(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tr}}+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{fl}})}
 =e^{\Delta\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tr}}}
  e^{\Delta\mathcal L_{\mathrm{fl}}}.
```

Every site permutation also commutes with every total-charge projector. Thus global charge projection can be moved through the specified transport semigroup, and the retained noise maps commute with it as well. These statements hold on the full spin density operator, not only on its diagonal.

This argument uses random permutations as the transport operations. A generic charge-conserving interacting Hamiltonian need not commute with independent bit-flip noise. Charge conservation alone does not justify the factorization.

## Allowed verification protocols

A deterministic protocol chooses a finite partition

```math
0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_m=T,\qquad
\Delta_i=t_i-t_{i-1}.
```

After each noisy interval, it performs the ideal nondestructive measurement $`\{\Pi_q\}_{q=0}^{2N}`$ of total occupation. It accepts a run exactly when every measured charge equals $`N`$. At the first other outcome, it rejects and stops that run. The final check at $`T`$ is included.

A check resolves total charge only. It does not measure the individual occupations or a finer syndrome. An accepted run may receive a final computational-basis measurement of the entire spin register. Arbitrary classical processing of that retained bitstring, the schedule label, and the global verification record is allowed.

The protocol does not introduce additional coherent encoding or recovery, correcting feedback, protected side memory, initial or intermediate microscopic measurements, a retained preparation bitstring, or microscopic readout of rejected runs. These exclusions define the late-readout observation contract. They are not conclusions of the theorem.

A randomized protocol may draw its schedule from external randomness independent of the input and unknown amplitude, with every schedule ending at the same $`T`$. Its schedule label may be retained. Choosing the next check using only previously successful outcomes $`Q=N`$ and external coins gives no additional accepted branch: those coins can equivalently be drawn in advance. Protocols that continue to process a failed run, use its microscopic state, or apply outcome-dependent corrections are different experiments.

Checks are instantaneous and noiseless in this model. A physical implementation must also pay for their synthesis, ancillas, measurements and duration. The attempt-count lower bounds already apply when these operations are ideal.

## What is charged

Every independently initialized run is one attempt, including every rejected run. A fresh input is consumed on each attempt. For a fixed schedule with acceptance probability $`P>0`$, obtaining one retained output requires $`1/P`$ attempts on average.

The information comparison counts information per initialized attempt. It does not count information per accepted shot while omitting the failures. A protocol may stop a failed attempt early; this can reduce the duration of that attempt, but does not erase its initialization or input consumption.

Physical evolution time, preparation, register size, global checks, terminal readout and classical processing are additional resource coordinates. The results do not identify an unrestricted optimal hardware runtime, preparation cost or thermodynamic cost.

The density-amplitude task grants a fresh state from the stated input family, passage through the duration-$`T`$ experiment, and the prescribed final readout. An early microscopic measurement followed by a protected classical record would change this contract and can bypass the noisy storage problem.

## Density modes and the input-amplitude family

For real coefficients $`c_j`$ with $`\sum_jc_j=0`$, write

```math
f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{L}c_j\left(x_j-\frac12\right).
```

We use the same symbol for the corresponding diagonal spin observable. The uniform component of the density is fixed on $`\Omega_N`$.

For a real nonzero Fourier mode on a periodic chain of length $`L\geq4`$, take $`c_j`$ proportional to a sine or cosine at $`k=2\pi\ell/L`$. The ideal adjoint evolution obeys

```math
e^{T\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tr}}^\dagger}f=h_k(T)f,\qquad
h_k(T)=e^{-\omega_kT},\qquad
\omega_k=2D(1-\cos k).
```

Indeed, the generator sends a linear coefficient vector to its nearest-neighbor discrete Laplacian. At the longest nonzero wavelength, $`\omega_k=\Theta(D/L^2)`$.

The density-amplitude experiment supplies independent fresh copies of

```math
\rho_u=\mu_N(I+uf),\qquad
\mu_N=\frac{\Pi_N}{\binom{2N}{N}}.
```

Fix $`0<\beta<1`$ and restrict the unknown amplitude to

```math
|u|\leq\frac{\beta}{\|f\|_\infty},\qquad
\|f\|_\infty=\max_{x\in\Omega_N}|f(x)|.
```

This gives a positive input law with a uniform denominator margin. The allowed amplitude interval can shrink with $`L`$ for an unnormalized extensive mode. Uniform finite-parameter claims must retain that dependence, or explicitly rescale the mode. The exact local information comparison is at $`u=0`$.

The full conditional-kernel result also covers the higher-degree difference spaces defined in [Conditional dynamics](conditional_dynamics.md). Their ideal evolution is generally an operator within the corresponding space, rather than one scalar Fourier decay rate.

## Limits of the physical conclusion

Exact total charge in every retained run is compatible with substantial residual loss of local density information. The question is how much global postselection can improve that information after all attempts are counted.

The result does not forbid inference of a transport coefficient by other observables, shorter times, calibrated rescaling or a separate fit for uniform noise decay. It does not establish a result for arbitrary noise, arbitrary quantum controls or every quantum transport model. In particular, [the coherent-control boundary](controls_and_limits.md#a-coherent-encoding-preserves-an-amplitude-at-every-size) gives an explicit escape when extra sector-preserving encoding is admitted.

The stochastic dynamics and symmetry projection are standard constructions. The contribution studied here is their exact cadence-dependent gain-versus-acceptance relation and the resulting comparison with a matched raw density experiment. Broader symmetry-verification and transport-extrapolation methods are credited in [Related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md).
